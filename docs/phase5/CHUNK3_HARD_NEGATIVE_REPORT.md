# Phase 5 Chunk 3: Hard Negative Generalization Report

## Overview
This report evaluates the model's adversarial robustness by analyzing the scoring of "True Hard Negatives"—entities specifically generated with `scenario_class = 'false_positive'` to mimic suspicious behavior without containing a genuine threat.

*(Note: A structural bug in the synthetic generator previously conflated normal entities with false-positive entities in the `is_false_positive` flag. The V2B and V2C metrics below have been explicitly filtered to `scenario_class = 'false_positive'` to remove the buggy dilution and measure true adversarial robustness).*

* **Total Hard Negatives (Test Split = 37,500):** 9,410 (Unfiltered canonical label)
* **Hard Negatives in Top 1% (Test Budget = 375):** 0
* **Hard Negatives in Top 5% (Test Budget = 1875):** 43
*(Source Note: Because V2A prediction scores were discarded after training, these counts were retrieved from historical logs. They represent the unfiltered, bug-diluted `is_false_positive` flag and cannot be re-filtered to the true `scenario_class` level).*

## True Score Distribution Analysis
When filtering out the bug-diluted entities, the true score distributions for the targeted adversarial population are revealed (V2B / V2C):
* **True Positives Mean Score:** 0.6391 / 0.6397
* **True Hard Negatives Mean Score:** 0.2978 / 0.3087
* **Ordinary Negatives (`normal` class) Mean Score:** 0.2203 / 0.2187

## Conclusion
**Robustness Against Synthetic Hard Negatives (Top-1% Budget):** The model correctly identifies that True Hard Negatives (mean score ~0.30) are more suspicious than ordinary normal people (mean score ~0.22). The generator succeeded in making them mathematically "harder." 

The model robustly isolates this synthetic adversarial noise from the critical decision boundary. Despite being more suspicious than normal entities, **0% of the true synthetic hard negatives penetrated the Top 1% alert budget in either unseen universe.** The highest-ranked adversarial entity safely landed at rank 1,301 (V2B) and 941 (V2C), well below the 500-entity cutoff. *(Note: V2C's maximum rank of 941 is identical to the earlier bug-diluted analysis, confirming that the single most-suspicious entity in the V2C pool was indeed a true `false_positive` scenario entity).*

**Top-5% Penetration Degradation:** However, at the Top-5% budget, the penetration rate is significantly worse than the bug-diluted analysis suggested. In V2B, the true penetration rate is 1.29% (32/2,471) compared to the diluted 0.38% (47/12,496) — roughly a 3.4x increase. In V2C, the true penetration rate is 1.79% (44/2,454) compared to the diluted 0.41% (52/12,645) — roughly a 4.4x increase. While the model perfectly protects the critical Top-1% budget, true synthetic adversaries slip into the wider Top-5% net at a much higher proportional rate than previously reported.

> [!WARNING]
> **Interpretation Limit:** As with the distribution shift findings, this demonstrates robustness against the specific synthetic adversarial patterns generated by the V2 simulator. It does **not** establish robustness against real-world adversarial actors actively attempting to evade the model.
